    Shannon's theorem states that the channel capacity (C) in bits per second (bps) is equal to the channel bandwidth (W) multiplied by the logarithm of the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) plus 1, where the logarithm is base 2. Mathematically, this can be expressed as:

    C = Wlog2(1 + S/N)

    The reason for this formula is that the channel capacity is limited by the amount of noise present in the channel. As the signal-to-noise ratio increases, more information can be transmitted through the channel, and the channel capacity increases. The logarithmic function is used because the relationship between the signal-to-noise ratio and the channel capacity is not linear, but rather logarithmic. The base 2 logarithm is used because digital signals are typically represented in binary form, and the base 2 logarithm is most appropriate for binary calculations.
